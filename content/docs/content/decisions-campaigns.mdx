Most content is written to attract traffic. **Decisions** campaigns are written for people who are already close to buying and need help choosing. Find them under **Content → Decisions**.

## How a Decisions campaign is built

Each campaign is built around one decision a buyer is trying to make, for example "which signage company should we use for our new branch?". It contains:

- **A hub page** that addresses the decision as a whole.
- **Spoke pages** that each answer one question buyers ask on the way. They come in four types:
  - **Clarifier:** explains something the buyer needs to understand.
  - **Use case:** shows how it works for a particular situation.
  - **Comparison:** compares options or alternatives honestly.
  - **Objection handler:** answers the worry that stops people deciding.
- **Sales enablement:** material for the sales team, such as an **objection sheet** and an **email sequence**.
- **Decision kit ideas:** suggestions for lead magnets, such as checklists, scorecards and calculators.

## Start a campaign

<Steps>
<Step>

### Open Decisions

Go to **Content → Decisions** and click **Start BDE**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Describe the decision

Fill in the **Campaign name**, the **Target persona**, the **Decision stage** (awareness, consideration or decision) and the **Target month**. Under **Buyer intent**, describe the decision the buyer is trying to make.

Not sure what to write? Click **Analyse Buyer Journey** and Verbolica suggests a **Decision Hub title** from the brand's data.

</Step>
<Step>

### Create and approve

Click **Create Campaign**. Review the planned hub and spokes, then click **Approve & Generate All Content** to have every piece written.

</Step>
<Step>

### Send it for approval

Use **Request approval (whole campaign)** to send the finished campaign to the client in one request.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Decision questions

Each campaign keeps a list of the real questions buyers ask, such as "how much does this cost for a 200-person company?". Add your own, or click **Suggest with AI**. The writing uses these questions, so they're worth getting right.

## Decision coverage

A good decision page covers six areas: **pricing**, **alternatives**, **risks**, **implementation**, **proof** and **fit**. The campaign shows which areas each page covers, so you can see gaps at a glance. Click **Re-grade campaign** after editing to update it.

## Measuring a Decisions campaign

Decisions campaigns are measured by **engagement**, not traffic. Once pages are live, the campaign shows:

- average reading time and bounce rate, compared with the brand's average
- time spent on the hub compared with the spokes
- how many visitors move from the hub to a spoke, and which spokes they choose
- form submissions on campaign pages

Engagement comes from the [WordPress plugin](/docs/connections/wordpress-plugin), so it works even without Google Analytics.

To compare campaigns side by side, open **Compare** from the Decisions page. To share results, use the print-ready **report** export.
